# Serial Tracker

A Firefox and Chrome extension for tracking web serials across sites. Track a story (or one story out of an author's mixed feed), and it learns the release schedule from the chapter dates, shows a progress meter toward the predicted next chapter, and checks shortly after that moment.

## Install

**Chrome** (116 or newer; also Edge, Brave and other Chromium browsers): open `chrome://extensions`, turn on *Developer mode*, press *Load unpacked* and choose this folder (the one that holds `manifest.json`). Chrome asks for the optional permissions (notifications, history, bookmarks, downloads, and sites other than the pre-approved ones) the first time a feature needs them.

**Firefox** (142 or newer): this folder's `manifest.json` is the Chrome one. For Firefox, replace it with `manifest.firefox.json` (keep a copy of the Chrome one) before loading or packaging the add-on.

### How the Chrome version differs

Chrome's background is a service worker, not Firefox's event page, so three things work differently under the hood. Nothing you see or do changes.
- **Reading pages.** A service worker has no DOM to parse HTML with, so the background hands each fetched page to a hidden *offscreen document* (`offscreen/`), which runs the same site readers (`lib/domops.js`) and returns the result as plain data. Firefox parses in the background itself.
- **Staying alive.** Chrome stops a service worker after about 30 seconds without activity. While a check run, import or other long job is going, the worker makes a cheap extension call every 20 seconds so it isn't stopped halfway. Between jobs it sleeps as normal, and the alarms wake it.
- **Replies.** The popup and dashboard get answers from the background through `sendResponse`, which Chrome requires (Firefox accepts it too).

## Use

- **Track something:** open a story page, click the toolbar button, press *Track*. Works from any page of the story (a chapter, `/reader/`, `page-3#post-…`).
- **Archives post late (Pawchive and similar):** an archive lists each post under the date the *author* uploaded it, but may only copy it days later. For these sources a post that isn't in the story yet counts as new whatever its date, so it is announced and counted as unread (if it arrived after your place was set; opening it clears it). The time each chapter was first seen here is kept, and once three chapters have been seen live, the schedule is shifted by the usual delay (*Details → Archive delay*), so *due*, *late* and the next check follow when chapters really appear here. Other sources are unchanged. Merged stories don't use this yet.
- **Pages that can't be read in the background (bot check or login):** by default the popup and dashboard offer a button that opens the page in front of you. To skip the asking, turn on *Open its page by itself…* in a story's details (or *Open it automatically from now on* on its notice), or Settings → *Open pages that can't be read in the background by themselves* for every story. A stopped check then opens the page in a background tab, one at a time, reads it once it has loaded, and always closes the tab. A tab still open after 3 minutes (a bot check that needs a click) is closed and skipped, and the button stays; the next scheduled check tries again.
- **Sorting the list:** *Filters and sort* → **Sort by** picks the order, with **Reverse the order** and **Then by** (a second key for stories that tie). Keys: by category (the default, with group headings), most unread, least unread, recently updated, next to update, favorites first, newest tracked, oldest tracked, title, author (stories without one go last), website, most chapters, recently checked. A story that ties on everything falls back to the by-category order. Reversing or adding a second key shows as a removable *Sort* pill under the toolbar. Your choices are remembered.
- **Filtering the list:** *Filters* (next to the search box) opens a panel with five groups: **Website** (each site your stories are on; a merged story counts for every site it is on), **Status** (ongoing, finished, abandoned), **Your place** (has unread chapters, caught up, place not known yet), **Schedule** (recently updated, due soon, coming up, due now, late, on a break, stopped reading, to read) and **Only stories that are** (favorites, silenced, paused, has a problem, merged). Choose any number of options. Within a group a story fits if it matches *any* choice; it must pass *every* group you chose in, except the last group, where a story must be *every* flag you pick. Each option shows how many stories you would see, and options that would show none are greyed out. Active filters appear as removable pills with a count of how many stories are shown, and your choices are remembered. Search covers titles, authors, site names and filters, and several words must all match. The old toolbar shortcuts (*Favorites only*, *With unread chapters*, *Hide finished and abandoned*, and the sort dropdown) are gone; use Favorites under *Only stories that are*, *Has unread chapters* under Your place, Status → Ongoing and Sort by. The current sort and filters stay visible as pills under the toolbar.
- **Patreon, without a membership:** open a creator's page (`patreon.com/c/<name>/posts`, `patreon.com/cw/<name>/posts`, or `patreon.com/<name>`) and press Track. A creator's post list shows every post's title and date to anyone, including posts locked to patrons, so new posts can be followed without paying. The creator's page gives the campaign id (kept after the first read), and the posts are read from Patreon's own post list, one small request per check. Use *Only posts matching* to follow just the chapters (for example `chapter, -poll`); the filter guide works the same as for author archives. Reading position is recorded when you open a post's address. Locked posts can't be read here, only noticed. Patreon isn't pre-approved: Firefox asks for access to patreon.com the first time. This reads Patreon's own page layout and post list, which Patreon can change, and it has not been tried against the live site.
- **Marinating:** a story that would be *on a break*, but showed a sign of life, is *marinating* instead. Signs of life are: a chapter that was edited, a post the story's filter leaves out (an announcement or poll from an author you follow), or a new chapter from *any* of a merged story's sources, even one another source already had. It lasts 21 days after the latest sign (Settings → Thresholds), then goes back to *on a break* unless something else happens. A marinating story says what happened and when. It isn't in Up next, since nothing is expected, and it can be filtered under Schedule.
- **Check schedule:** the *Check schedule* button at the top of the dashboard lists every story's next planned check, soonest first, grouped as due now, in the next hour, later today, tomorrow, this week and later, with the time, how long until it, why that time was picked (waiting for the predicted time, the expected chapter isn't up yet, long overdue, on a break, trying again after a problem, still learning) and when it was last checked. Stories that are paused or finished are listed as not scheduled. *Check now* runs one check immediately. The list refreshes while it is open.
- **Up next and the filter:** the toolbar popup's Up next and the dashboard's Up next both follow the dashboard's filter (Filters and sort), so the same stories show in both. The popup can't change it: its *Filter: …* button opens the dashboard with the filter panel open. The search box isn't part of it.
- **Scribble Hub:** open a series page (`/series/…`) or any of its chapters (`/read/…/chapter/…`) and press Track. Routine checks read the series' own feed (one small request); the first read also reads the series page's table of contents for older chapters, and falls back to that page if the feed is missing or empty. Reading position, browsing-history lookup and bookmark-folder import work as they do for Royal Road. Your Scribble Hub reading list (`scribblehub.com/reading-list/`) can be imported like Royal Road follows: from the page itself (toolbar button) or from the Import stories dialog. It is read from the page's own markup and has not been tried on every layout of that page.
- **Missed days on the calendar:** a red square means the story was expected to release that day, nothing came, and the moment the add-on would have looked has passed (the usual time plus the first-look delay in Settings; for a date-only source, once the date is over). A miss is only drawn while a release is still expected: for the larger of 14 days and three usual gaps after a chapter (where the dashboard says *On a break?*). A long hiatus is a break, not hundreds of missed days. Days after the newest chapter count as they pass, future days never do. The squares only describe the past on the calendar; they don't change when anything is checked.
- **Dates instead of times:** a source that only gives dates (Archive of Our Own, NovelUpdates, Webnovel) is shown in days (*today*, *yesterday*, *3 days ago*, *Expected Fri, Oct 30*, *Late by 2 days*), the progress meter moves a day at a time, and checks are by date: the first look early on the expected date, two more that day, then once a day.
- **FanFiction.net (story updates only):** its chapters have no dates, only the story's *Updated* time. A different *Updated* time than the one on record is one new update (named by its chapter count), and the schedule is learned from the updates seen. Track it from the story's own page; the add-on says so before you press Track. Opening any page of the story counts as reading its newest update.
- **NovelUpdates** (series page release table) and **Webnovel** (`/book/<id>/catalog`) are read as date-only sources. Not tried against the live sites (reconstructed markup). NovelUpdates releases lead off-site, so opening a chapter can't be recognised; opening the story from the add-on clears its new count.
- **Lists you can import from:** Archive of Our Own (bookmarks, subscriptions, history), Webnovel library, NovelUpdates reading list and series finder, Wattpad library and lists, FanFiction.net favorites and author pages, Patreon memberships, Pawchive/Kemono favorites, in addition to Royal Road, Scribble Hub and forum watched threads.
- **Expected date:** the day shown as *Expected* is never behind today. When a chapter doesn't come, the headline keeps counting how late it is (from the original prediction) and the line under it moves to the *Next expected* day (the next active weekday, or the next usual gap). The calendar's dashed *expected* square moves with it.
- **Links:** every web link in the popup and dashboard opens as a normal browser tab, never inside the add-on page.
- **Any other website:** open the page that lists the story's chapters and press *Track*. The add-on looks for the list in this order and uses the first that works: (1) **rows of the page** that each have a link and a date (table rows, list items, a date heading above a post, a date written next to a link); (2) **data in the page** (JSON-LD, Next.js/Nuxt state, an Apollo cache, or a script such as `window.chapters = [...]`), for sites that build the list with script; (3) **the site's RSS/Atom feed**, read from inside the page, when the page lists no dates at all (most WordPress, Blogger, Substack and Ghost sites). If several lists are found the popup lets you pick one and says when a list doesn't look like chapters. Dates can be written as `2026-10-23`, `2026/10/23`, `23.10.2026`, `October 23, 2026`, `23 octobre 2026` (English, Spanish, Portuguese, French, German, Italian, Dutch and Indonesian months), `2026年10月23日`, `Oct 23` (no year: the latest such day that isn't in the future), or relative (`3 hours ago`, `yesterday`). A relative date is worked out when the page is read, so the first reading of a chapter is the one kept. A time with no zone is read in your browser's zone; for Japanese, Chinese, Korean and Taiwanese sites (`.jp`, `.cn`, `.kr`, `.tw`, `syosetu.com`) it is read in that country's zone. A list split over several pages (`?page=3`, `?p=3`, `/page/3/`) and ordered oldest first is read from its **last page**, where the newest chapters are. If the feed is the source it holds every post, so use *Only posts matching* to follow just the chapters. **Limits:** a list that shows no dates and has no feed can't be tracked (a schedule can't be learned without dates); pages built entirely by script that carry no data the add-on recognises, and lists loaded by a separate request after the page opens (some Madara-theme sites), appear empty to a background read; a page with only one row can't be told from other lists until a story is tracked.
- **Archive of Our Own:** open a work (any chapter, or the work page) and press Track. The chapter index (`/works/<id>/navigate`) is read: it lists every chapter with its date, but not its time. Reading position is recorded when you open a chapter. Only the date is known, so the schedule is learned to the day.
- **Wattpad:** open a story's page (`wattpad.com/story/<id>-…`; a chapter's address names the chapter, not the story) and press Track. The chapters and their dates come from the story's own data on Wattpad (`api/v3/stories/<id>`, one small request per check); drafts are left out. This follows the same data WebToEpub and FanFicFare use, and has not been tried against the live site.
- **Author feeds (Pawchive and similar):** an author posts chapters, polls and announcements in one feed. Put a filter in *Only posts matching* and only those posts count for predictions and "new" alerts. The popup shows how many posts on the current page match as you type. Next to the box, **How filters work** is a short guide: click an example to fill the box, and a live list of the page's latest posts shows which ones the filter keeps (✓) or ignores (✗). The same guide is in a story's *Details* when you edit its filter.
  - `chapter` any title containing it (case-insensitive)
  - `chapter, interlude` any of several
  - `chapter, -poll` exclude with a leading `-`
  - `/^(chapter\s*)?\d+\s*:/` a regular expression (this one matches both "Chapter 355: …" and "351: …")
- **Dashboard:** *Open dashboard* in the popup (or Preferences for the add-on in `about:addons`). It lists everything with progress meters and an **Up next** strip. Sort and filter it under Filters and sort. Filter to favorites, to stories with new chapters, or hide finished and abandoned ones. The dashboard is an extension page served locally from the add-on, so it can read your data without any server.
- **Where you are:** each story shows *You’re on Chapter N* and how many chapters come after it. Unread means after your place, never "new since I added it", and opening a story's page does not mark anything read.
  - **Re-reading an earlier chapter:** your place only moves forward by itself. If the chapter you opened most recently is *before* your place, the story offers *Move my place to it* (or *Keep my place*), in the popup and in the dashboard. It disappears once you read further, set your place, or choose either button. It works out your place by itself:
  - **Royal Road, Scribble Hub, Pawchive-style archives:** opening a chapter page (for about 4 seconds) puts you on that chapter.
  - **Long threadmark lists (XenForo):** a list is split into pages (for example 25 or 50 chapters each). The first read of a thread takes the pages that hold the newest 250 chapters, the most the tracker keeps, newest page first, so a 100+ chapter story is complete instead of just its first and last page. Routine checks still read only the last page. A story read by an earlier version gets this one-time catch-up on its next check; the older chapters it fills in never count as new.
  - **XenForo threads:** how far down the thread you have scrolled. The date of the furthest post you reached decides which chapter you are on (reader mode and ordinary pages both work). It only moves forward.
  - **Browsing history (optional, asked once):** for stories you were already reading before you tracked them. Royal Road and archives are exact (it matches the chapters you opened). XenForo uses a post you opened if there is one; otherwise the last page you were on, shown as "around" because you may not have finished it.
  - **Not known yet** is shown as such, with *Set it* to choose a chapter (or *I’m caught up*). Only you can move your place backwards.
  - If you open a chapter before the next check has seen it, that is remembered and resolved when it appears, so a chapter you read the moment it came out never shows as unread.
- **To read:** a story whose place isn't known yet (you haven't opened any of its chapters, and haven't set a place) is tagged *To read* and listed under its own *To read* heading, right after *Stopped reading*. Like stopped reading it is left out of *Up next*, and it can be picked in *Filters → Schedule*. It moves out as soon as your place is known. Badge and notification counts are not affected.
- **Finished and abandoned:** *Details → Status* tags a story *Finished* or *Abandoned* (the default is *Ongoing*). It is shown under its own heading at the bottom, dimmed, and is never checked, counted on the badge, listed in Up next or notified about. Set it back to *Still following* and checking resumes. Works on merged stories too.
- **Check a favorite more often:** *Details → Check this favorite more often* (shown for favorites only, off by default). It looks about a minute after the predicted time instead of twenty, retries every 10 to 30 minutes at first instead of every 30 minutes to a few hours, and, once an expected chapter is long overdue, looks again at least every 12 hours. A favorite on a break follows the same break rules as any other story. It costs more requests to that site, so use it sparingly.
- **Jump to a story's details:** each Up next entry has a button (in the toolbar popup and on the dashboard) that opens that story's expanded details in the dashboard's list, scrolled into view. Any filters or search that would hide it are cleared first.
- **Check now:** *Check all now* (popup or dashboard), or the refresh button on a row.
- **Backups:** *Save backup* and *Restore backup* at the top of the dashboard. A backup holds every story (with your place and history, status and merges) and your settings, including the theme and colors. Restoring never overwrites stories you already track, and says how many it restored, skipped as already tracked, or couldn't read. (*Import stories* is a different thing: it adds stories from a site's list or a bookmarks folder, see below.)
  - **Automatic backups (off by default):** Settings → *Data and privacy* → *Save a backup automatically*, every day, week or month. A full backup is saved to `Downloads/Serial Tracker backups/`. Needs the Downloads permission, asked when you turn it on. *Save backup now* does it on demand, and the last backup time is shown.
  - **Settings only:** *Save settings* (same place) saves just your settings to `serial-tracker-settings.json`, without any stories. *Load settings* loads it, or the settings from a full backup, and leaves your stories alone. The defaults the add-on starts with are in `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` in `lib/model.js`.
- **Import stories:** add many stories at once from a list you already keep. It only ever runs when you start it from this dialog; nothing import-related runs in the background otherwise (an import you started continues if the add-on is unloaded, and stops when it finishes or when you stop it).
  - **From a site's list, in the dashboard:** *Import stories* → pick Royal Road *Follows / Favorites / Read later* or a SpaceBattles / SufficientVelocity / QuestionableQuesting *Watched threads* page, or paste any list address. It reads every page of the list (up to 15), shows what it found with the ones you already track greyed out, and adds what you tick.
  - **From a Firefox bookmarks folder:** *Or import a Firefox bookmarks folder* → allow Bookmarks (asked once) → pick a folder → *Read this folder*. The folder and its subfolders are scanned for Royal Road stories and forum threads, which are listed for you to tick. Other bookmarks are skipped and counted. Sites that aren't already allowed are asked for when you press Import.
  - **Watched threads:** a forum's `/watched/threads` page normally lists only threads with unread posts, so an import always asks for the full list (`?unread=0`), even from the page you have open or an address typed without it. If the full list can't be read in the background, it falls back to the page you had and says that only unread threads are listed.
  - **From the page itself:** on a list page (for example `royalroad.com/my/follows` or a forum's `/watched/threads`), the toolbar popup offers *Choose and import…*. It reads the page you have open, so it also works when the site needs a login or a bot check that background requests can't pass.
  - Options: mark all as favorites; mark all as caught up (otherwise each story's place is unknown until you open a chapter).
  - Adding is paced like any other check (one story at a time, with the usual gap between requests to a site). A forum thread without Threadmarks has no chapter list to read, so it's reported and skipped. Watched threads, forum lists and bookmark folders are mixed content, so only a list you built yourself (watched threads, Royal Road follows, a bookmarks folder) starts fully ticked.
- **How many Up next:** Settings → *Number of stories in Up next* sets how many stories the toolbar popup (1–15) and the dashboard (1–24) show. Stories that are *late* always come after the ones that are due or coming up.
- **Quiet hours:** Settings → *Toolbar badge and notifications* → *Quiet hours* (off by default). Between the hours you choose, notifications are delayed; checking continues and the badge still updates. When quiet hours end, the chapters found are notified together, except those you have read in the meantime.
- **Appearance:** Settings → *Theme, colors and list layout*. Themes (Default, which follows your system's light or dark setting and is what a fresh install uses; Light; Dark; Gray; Black; High contrast), page backgrounds (plain, dots, grid, ruled paper, soft gradient, or your own page color), and a color for each status: coming up, due soon, due now, late, on a break, favorite star, caught up, unread badge (which also colors the toolbar badge). Every color starts as the theme's own; *Reset all colors to the theme's own* undoes your picks.
- **Restore defaults:** every section of Settings ends with *Restore defaults for this section*, which puts only that section back to the add-on's own defaults. The defaults are `DEFAULT_SETTINGS` in `lib/model.js`.
- **Permissions, and why:** Firefox shows its own prompt for each permission and the add-on can't add text to it, so the reason is written next to the button that asks, and repeated if you decline. Settings → *What each permission is for* lists them all with whether each is allowed now. In short: **site access** to read a tracked story's chapter list; **notifications** to announce new chapters; **browsing history** to find chapters you already opened (searched on your device, nothing saved or sent); **bookmarks** to read the one folder you pick; **downloads** to write backup files. Nothing is sent anywhere except requests to sites you track.

## Firefox for Android

The add-on declares Android support (`gecko_android`, Firefox 142+). On a touch screen it uses larger tap targets, 16px fields so the page doesn't zoom, a full-screen settings and import dialog, and a popup that fills the screen width. Open it from the Firefox menu → *Extensions* → Serial Tracker; *Open dashboard* in the popup opens the full dashboard in a tab.

Optional features that may not exist in Firefox for Android (browsing history, bookmarks, downloads, notifications) fail with a message saying so instead of "wasn't allowed". Without history, your place comes from opening chapters or *Set it*; use *Save backup* instead of automatic backups.

Not yet run on a device. Android can stop background processes, so automatic checks may be less punctual than on desktop. The manifest is MV3; Mozilla's Android guidance prefers MV2, so if the add-on won't install or `scripting` calls fail on Android, that is the first suspect.

**Testing it on a phone (USB):**
1. On the phone: enable *Developer options* and *USB debugging*. In Firefox (Nightly or Beta is best for testing): *Settings → Advanced → Remote debugging via USB*.
2. On the computer: install `adb` (Android platform-tools) and `web-ext` (`npm install -g web-ext`). Connect the phone, allow the USB-debugging prompt, and run `adb devices`: your phone should be listed.
3. Unzip the add-on, then from that folder run `web-ext lint`, then `web-ext run -t firefox-android --adb-device <id from adb devices> --firefox-apk org.mozilla.fenix` (Nightly; `org.mozilla.firefox` is release, `org.mozilla.firefox_beta` is Beta; leave `--firefox-apk` off to choose from a list).
4. Firefox on the phone restarts with the add-on loaded temporarily (it goes away when `web-ext` stops). To debug, open `about:debugging` in desktop Firefox, connect to the device, and press *Inspect* on Serial Tracker for the background console.
5. For a lasting install without AMO, use Firefox Nightly with a custom add-on collection (see Mozilla's Extension Workshop guide to developing for Firefox for Android).

## How checking works

| Mode (Settings) | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| **Right after each predicted update** (default) | No checks until a chapter is expected. Then one check about 20 minutes after the predicted time, and further checks about 1 h, 3 h, 6 h, 12 h, 1 day, 2 days and 4 days after it if nothing has been posted (minutes late, hours late, days late). When a chapter appears, there are no more checks until the next predicted time. A story is never checked before its predicted time. About 5 days after the window opened the chapter is no longer chased: from then on the story is looked at again only as often as Settings says (1 week by default; *Never* means only when you press Check). Stories still learning their schedule (no prediction yet) are checked once a week, favorites included. |
| **Stories on a break** | A story that is *on a break* is checked rarely. If it showed a sign of life (*marinating*): once a week. Otherwise, if its last chapter is under about 6 months old: once a month. Otherwise (no chapter for about 6 months): never automatically; only *Check now* / *Check all now* look at it, and so does opening its page yourself. This also applies to retries after a problem and to favorites set to *check more often*. |
| **Only when I press Check** | Never automatic. |

Always true:
- Plain background requests: no tabs, no windows, no focus changes.
- **When a bot check or login wall stops a background request** it tries these steps in order and stops at the first one that works:
  1. **Use a tab you already have open** on that site. The request is made from inside that page (same cookies, same container, same origin), so the site sees the same session as that tab. No tab is opened, moved or reloaded.
  2. **Open the exact page it was trying to read.** The story gets an *Open it to pass the check* button (and the dashboard a banner, the popup a footer button) that opens that specific URL, including `?page=N` for the newest page of a long Threadmarks list. Once the page has loaded, its HTML is read from the page itself, just as for any page you visit, and the tab closes (Settings can turn that off). With several stories blocked, *Open them one at a time* opens them in turn, and closing a tab yourself skips that page. Only tabs the add-on opened are ever closed.
  3. **Adding a blocked story still adds it**, waiting for its first read, instead of failing.
  After you pass the check once, later background checks usually succeed.
- **Sites are checked side by side.** Each site is its own lane: requests to the *same* site go one at a time with at least 5 seconds between them (Settings: 3 to 60 seconds), while different sites are checked at the same time (up to six at once). Three sites with three stories each take about as long as one site with three, not nine.  `429`/`Retry-After` and bot-check pages cause a long back-off and are never counted as "the author missed a chapter".
- After a long absence (browser closed for days) it checks at most 4 stories per site, and 24 in all, per round instead of everything at once, so one big site can't crowd out the rest.
- **Everything is local.** Checks run inside the browser (an event page woken by alarms). There is no server, account or cloud service, and nothing is sent anywhere except the requests to sites you track. The trade-off is that checks only happen while the browser is open; after it has been closed, it catches up shortly after it starts.
- **Site feeds as notifications.** Web Push from a site goes to the browser, not to extensions, so an extension cannot receive it. What it can use is the site's RSS/Atom feed: if a story has one (Royal Road's is auto-detected, or paste one under *Details → Site feed*), automatic checks first ask the feed whether anything changed, using `ETag`/`If-Modified-Since`, usually answered with an empty `304`. The page is only read when the feed changes (and once a week regardless).
- **Reading the page yourself counts.** When you open the page that lists a tracked story's chapters, the add-on reads the chapter list from that page and counts it as a check. It only ever looks at pages of stories you track.
- Requests use the browser's own cookies, so logins and bot-check clearance apply. No headers are faked.
- Permissions are per site: the sites you named are pre-approved; any other site prompts once when you track something there. Notifications (one summary per automatic round, held during quiet hours if you set them), browsing history, bookmarks and downloads (automatic backups) each have their own optional permission, asked when you first use the feature. Notifications are switched on in Settings by default but show nothing until you allow the permission. The toolbar badge shows unread chapters.

## Tests

The Node test suite for the generic reader is not part of the add-on, so it comes as a separate download (`serial-tracker-tests`). Unzip it so that its `tests/` folder sits next to `manifest.json`, then `cd tests && npm install && npm test`.

- `node run.js` runs the generic reader over `fixtures/` (one page per site or layout) and says which lists it finds; `node run.js baseline-adapters.js` does the same for the reader as it was in 1.19.1.
- `node dates.test.js` checks the date reader (79 cases, including strings that must be rejected). `node integration.test.js` checks saved stories, multi-page lists, feeds, Archive of Our Own, Wattpad, the existing sites and speed.
- `node build-fixtures.js` rebuilds the fixtures. Each fixture's provenance is in `cases.js`: *verified* (markup confirmed in WebToEpub, FanFicFare or this add-on's own adapters), *reconstructed* (from knowledge of the site, not compared with the live site), or *synthetic* (a pattern, not a copy of one site). A pass means the reader copes with that markup, not that the live site hasn't changed.
- Run them with different `TZ=` values: times with no zone are read in the browser's zone.

`docs/webtoepub-sites.md` lists the sites WebToEpub supports, grouped by the engine they run on.
